Personality Semantic Atlas

Every personality system ever written down describes the same handful of people in different words. This is a map of that overlap — trait-profiles from Theophrastus to HEXACO, from the Chinese zodiac to phrenology, placed by what they actually say rather than by who said it.

Cooked

The traits arranged into six family squares — the four classical humors plus Competence and Mystical — each with its own deliberate axes, and transition bands where families blend. The frame is imposed; where each trait sits inside it is earned from the data.

Harvested

The same traits with the frame taken away. Each profile’s vocabulary is embedded and projected to two dimensions, so distance means dissimilarity and nothing else. The clusters, the axis words, and the empty valleys between regions were all found, not chosen.

What you can do in either map

  • Click any trait for its full profile — definition, motivation, behaviors, strengths, origins — plus its nearest relatives and the adjectives it shares with them.
  • Compare two traits, or lasso two whole regions, side by side — including the words they don’t literally share but mean the same by.
  • Lasso any area to harvest the adjectives that define it, ranked by how distinctive they are to that region.
  • Convergence recolours the map by how many independent systems describe a trait the same way — the closest thing here to a claim about what’s real.

A caution about what this is

The systems mapped here are not equally credible. Phrenology and the zodiac are included as historical objects — things people believed about character — alongside instruments with real psychometric standing like HEXACO and the NEO facets. Placing them on one map is a claim about vocabulary, not about validity. Where distinct traditions independently land in the same place, that convergence is worth noticing; it is not, on its own, evidence that any one of them is true.
