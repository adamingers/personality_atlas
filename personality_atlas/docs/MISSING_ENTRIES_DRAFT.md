## Part 1 — Cattell's 16PF, factors 11–16 (continue after "10. Abstractedness (Factor M)")

11. Privateness (Factor N)
Definition: Tendency toward discretion, social shrewdness, and non-disclosure versus forthright openness and artless genuineness.
High adjectives: private, discreet, guarded, shrewd, polished, diplomatic, calculating, astute, worldly, non-disclosing.
Low adjectives: forthright, genuine, open, artless, transparent, unpretentious, candid, naive, direct, self-revealing.
Short phrases: “Keeps own counsel” vs. “Wears heart on sleeve.”
Underlying Motivation: High → protect the self through strategic self-presentation; Low → connect through unfiltered honesty.
Permanence: Moderately stable; sharpened by social experience and settings that reward discretion.
Observable Behaviors: High → deflecting personal questions, careful impression management; Low → volunteering personal details, speaking without calculation.
Strengths & Downsides: High → tact, diplomacy, social skill; Low → authenticity and approachability, but risk of oversharing or being outmaneuvered.
Developmental Origins: Shaped by whether openness was met with acceptance or exploitation; cultures and families that prize propriety.

12. Apprehension (Factor O)
Definition: Proneness to worry, self-doubt, and guilt versus untroubled self-assurance and security.
High adjectives: apprehensive, worried, self-doubting, guilt-prone, insecure, self-blaming, brooding, anxious, sensitive, fretful.
Low adjectives: self-assured, secure, unworried, confident, untroubled, complacent, self-satisfied, serene, resilient, unruffled.
Short phrases: “Expects to be blamed” vs. “Rarely second-guesses self.”
Underlying Motivation: High → avoid error and censure through vigilant self-scrutiny; Low → act freely from a settled sense of adequacy.
Permanence: Moderately stable, with strong links to trait anxiety; responsive to life stress and therapy.
Observable Behaviors: High → apologizing readily, replaying mistakes, seeking reassurance; Low → shrugging off criticism, sleeping easily after setbacks.
Strengths & Downsides: High → conscience, sensitivity to error; Low → composure and confidence, but risk of complacency or blind spots.
Developmental Origins: Critical or perfectionistic caregiving, early experiences of blame versus unconditional acceptance.

13. Openness to Change (Factor Q1)
Definition: Experimenting, questioning orientation toward new ideas versus attachment to the traditional and familiar.
High adjectives: experimenting, liberal, questioning, analytical, freethinking, flexible, innovative, critical, curious, progressive.
Low adjectives: traditional, conservative, conventional, respectful, familiar-loving, cautious, established, loyal, moralistic, resistant.
Short phrases: “Asks why not” vs. “Trusts the tried and true.”
Underlying Motivation: High → improve things through inquiry and reform; Low → preserve what has proven safe and workable.
Permanence: Fairly stable disposition; overlaps Openness to Experience and liberal-conservative attitude dimensions.
Observable Behaviors: High → challenging procedures, adopting new methods early; Low → defending customs, preferring established routines.
Strengths & Downsides: High → adaptability, reformist energy; Low → stability and continuity, but risk of rigidity or being left behind.
Developmental Origins: Exposure to diverse ideas and rewarded questioning versus environments prizing obedience and tradition.

14. Self-Reliance (Factor Q2)
Definition: Preference for depending on one's own judgment and resources versus orientation toward group support and joint action.
High adjectives: self-reliant, solitary, individualistic, resourceful, independent, self-sufficient, autonomous, private, self-directed, lone-working.
Low adjectives: group-oriented, affiliative, joining, consulting, companionable, dependent, team-minded, sociable, consensus-seeking, follower-like.
Short phrases: “Decides things alone” vs. “Prefers to consult the group.”
Underlying Motivation: High → maintain autonomy and trust one's own counsel; Low → gain security and direction through belonging.
Permanence: Stable style of working and deciding; distinct from introversion though often correlated.
Observable Behaviors: High → working solo, resisting committee decisions; Low → seeking opinions, thriving on collaboration.
Strengths & Downsides: High → independence, decisiveness; Low → cooperation and support networks, but risk of dependence or conformity.
Developmental Origins: Early necessity or encouragement of self-sufficiency versus strong communal or family embeddedness.

15. Perfectionism (Factor Q3)
Definition: Drive toward order, self-discipline, and exacting standards versus tolerance of disorder and casual flexibility.
High adjectives: perfectionistic, organized, self-disciplined, exacting, precise, controlled, compulsive, thorough, fastidious, goal-bound.
Low adjectives: flexible, casual, unexacting, spontaneous, disorderly, lax, easygoing, improvising, tolerant, undisciplined.
Short phrases: “Everything in its place” vs. “Lets things stay loose.”
Underlying Motivation: High → achieve control and self-respect through meeting internal standards; Low → stay free of constraining rules and routines.
Permanence: Stable regulatory style; strengthens with roles demanding precision.
Observable Behaviors: High → detailed planning, checking work repeatedly, tidy environments; Low → improvised schedules, comfort amid clutter.
Strengths & Downsides: High → reliability, high-quality output; Low → spontaneity and adaptability, but risk of carelessness or missed standards.
Developmental Origins: Socialization around order and achievement; internalization of parental or cultural standards of correctness.

16. Tension (Factor Q4)
Definition: Restless, driven energy and impatience versus relaxed, placid tranquility.
High adjectives: tense, driven, impatient, restless, frustrated, wound-up, irritable, time-pressured, edgy, overwrought.
Low adjectives: relaxed, placid, tranquil, patient, composed, calm, unhurried, easygoing, serene, low-key.
Short phrases: “Runs on nervous energy” vs. “Takes life at ease.”
Underlying Motivation: High → discharge accumulated drive and meet pressing demands; Low → conserve equilibrium and avoid needless strain.
Permanence: Fluctuates with situational stress around a stable set-point of arousability.
Observable Behaviors: High → fidgeting, snapping under delay, working at high pitch; Low → waiting comfortably, steady unhurried pace.
Strengths & Downsides: High → urgency, productivity under pressure; Low → composure and patience, but risk of insufficient drive.
Developmental Origins: Temperamental arousability interacting with chronic demand, overload, or frustration of needs.

## Part 2 — Eysenck's PEN model (insert as pole blocks alongside High/Low Psychoticism)

High Extraversion (High E)
Definition: A sociable, lively, and stimulation-seeking disposition, marked by assertiveness, high activity, and carefree surgency.
Adjectives: sociable, lively, active, assertive, sensation-seeking, carefree, dominant, venturesome, talkative, energetic.
Short phrases: “Comes alive around people” • “Seeks excitement constantly” • “Takes the social lead” • “Acts first, reflects later” • “Craves stimulation and change.”
Underlying Motivation: Raise chronically low cortical arousal by pursuing social contact, novelty, and excitement.
Permanence: A biologically rooted temperament (Eysenck tied it to under-arousal of the reticulo-cortical system); highly stable across the lifespan.
Observable Behaviors: Initiating conversations, filling calendars with social events, seeking parties and adventure, quick decisions, dominating groups.
Strengths & Downsides:
Strengths: Social ease, leadership, optimism, energy, breadth of contacts.
Downsides: Impulsivity, distractibility, sensation-seeking risk, shallow attention to solitary work.
Developmental Origins: Heritable arousal set-point shaped by rewarded sociability and stimulating early environments.

Low Extraversion (Introversion) (Low E)
Definition: A quiet, reserved, and reflective disposition that favors solitude, depth, and controlled deliberate action.
Adjectives: quiet, reserved, reflective, introspective, controlled, deliberate, solitary, thoughtful, calm, self-contained.
Short phrases: “Recharges in solitude” • “Thinks before acting” • “Prefers few deep bonds” • “Avoids overstimulation” • “Keeps own company happily.”
Underlying Motivation: Protect a high baseline of cortical arousal by limiting stimulation and choosing depth over breadth.
Permanence: Stable temperamental style with strong biological grounding; expression varies by context.
Observable Behaviors: Choosing quiet settings, working alone for long stretches, listening more than speaking, planning before acting.
Strengths & Downsides:
Strengths: Concentration, depth, independence, considered judgment.
Downsides: Social hesitance, under-assertion, isolation risk, being overlooked in groups.
Developmental Origins: Heritable over-arousal tendencies reinforced by environments rewarding quiet application.

High Neuroticism (High N)
Definition: An emotionally reactive disposition prone to anxiety, moodiness, and distress under stress.
Adjectives: anxious, moody, tense, irritable, worried, emotional, guilt-prone, shy, volatile, vulnerable.
Short phrases: “Feels everything intensely” • “Worries past the danger” • “Moods shift quickly” • “Stress leaves deep marks” • “Braced for the worst.”
Underlying Motivation: Detect and avert threat through heightened emotional vigilance.
Permanence: A stable, heritable temperament (Eysenck linked it to limbic system reactivity); moderates somewhat with age.
Observable Behaviors: Rumination, strong reactions to criticism, sleep disturbance under stress, frequent reassurance-seeking, avoidance of feared situations.
Strengths & Downsides:
Strengths: Sensitivity, threat detection, empathy for suffering, motivation to prepare.
Downsides: Chronic distress, health strain, indecision, relationship friction, vulnerability to anxiety and mood disorders.
Developmental Origins: Heritable limbic reactivity amplified by early adversity, unpredictable environments, or anxious modeling.

Low Neuroticism (Emotional Stability) (Low N)
Definition: A calm, even-tempered, and resilient disposition that recovers quickly from stress and rarely overreacts.
Adjectives: calm, stable, even-tempered, composed, resilient, unworried, secure, steady, relaxed, imperturbable.
Short phrases: “Weathers storms steadily” • “Recovers quickly from setbacks” • “Keeps emotions in proportion” • “Hard to rattle” • “Even keel throughout.”
Underlying Motivation: Engage the world from a settled emotional baseline rather than from threat-management.
Permanence: A stable temperament reflecting low limbic reactivity; among the most heritable of dispositions.
Observable Behaviors: Staying composed in crises, sleeping well under pressure, measured responses to provocation, quick return to baseline after upset.
Strengths & Downsides:
Strengths: Resilience, clear judgment under stress, reliability, soothing presence for others.
Downsides: May underestimate real threats, appear unmoved or unempathetic, miss warning signals others feel.
Developmental Origins: Heritable low reactivity supported by secure attachment and predictable early environments.
